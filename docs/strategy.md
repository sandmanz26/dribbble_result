# Strategy notes

Brief (owner, as head of creative, design and marketing): two matcha websites, one fun and one minimal, 4 to 5 home sections, homepage, about and product page. Show the matcha's credibility and quality, and give a feeling of calm, peace and enjoyment. Reference for the fun one: coupletcoffee.com (bright colour blocks, hand-drawn type, products on colour backdrops), used for spirit only.

Fun but calm: colour and bouncy type carry the fun; soft sunlight, slow page colour changes and space carry the calm. Credibility: one farm in Wazuka, shading, first harvest, stone mill, whisked to order, tested each lot (samples to confirm with a real client).

## Roast (what could go wrong, and the fix)
- AI garbles small text on cans and tins: one large short wordmark only, no small print, never upscale.
- Can changes shape between shots: generate the packshot first and pass it as the reference to every can image.
- Matcha comes out neon or yellow: say "vivid jade green, natural, not neon".
- Iced latte layers look muddy: "green matcha layer floating over white milk, clear glass".
- Hands with wrong fingers: crop tight, hands relaxed, avoid many fingers in frame.
- Faces read uncanny and people could look like real public figures: back views, side, out of focus.
- Tea field looks like generic hills: must show black shade cloth over rows (kabuse), that is the credibility.
- Stone mill drawn wrong: "two round grey granite stones stacked, wooden handle, fine green powder at the rim".
- Fun site copying the reference's neon pink and purple: use our own palette and calm light.
- Signage text in the cafe: "no signs, no text".
- Aspect ratios chosen for the layout first (heroes 16:9 or 4:5, products 4:5, backgrounds 3:2).
