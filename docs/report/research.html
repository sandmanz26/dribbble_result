Headspace

Meditation · market leader

The easiest meditation app to recommend to a beginner: a guided path, a friendly voice and a distinctive illustrated look.

Price$12.99 / mo, $69.99 / yr, $399.99 lifetime, 7-day trial
Rating4.8 from about 1M App Store reviews (July 2025)
Best forComplete beginners who do not want to browse

Strengths

  • Visual design reviewers call distinctive and genuinely good; easy to navigate.
  • Clear, professional narration, much of it by co-founder Andy Puddicombe, gives the app one trusted voice.
  • A structured path removes the “what should I do” problem for beginners.
  • Personalisation has improved, using stated goals and usage.

Weaknesses

  • Personalisation is “better than average but not deep”; it follows your history, not how you feel today.
  • Engagement with mindfulness apps drops sharply after week one; streaks and badges only partly help.
  • Not built for clinical needs such as anxiety disorders or depression.
  • Built around meditation; no herbal guidance.
Tend takes: one obvious next step and one consistent voice.
Tend avoids: streaks and badges as the answer to week-two drop-off.

Calm

Meditation and sleep

The sleep app of the category: Sleep Stories read by well-known actors, plus meditation, breathing, music and movement.

PriceFree tier; Premium price varies by offer
Best forFalling asleep
ContentSleep Stories, box and belly breathing, soundscapes, kids’ content

Strengths

  • Sleep Stories are the standout, with narrators such as Matthew McConaughey and Cillian Murphy.
  • Easy-to-use interface and regular updates (Healthline); highly customisable, varied content (TechRadar).
  • Includes guided breathing techniques such as box breathing and deep exhales.
  • Family-friendly content for children.

Weaknesses

  • Somewhat expensive with a short trial; the free version has far fewer features.
  • No sleep monitoring, despite being the sleep app.
  • Stories can run too long and nature sounds can distract.
  • Some options are hidden; no social features.
Tend takes: an evening mode that ends the day softly.
Tend avoids: a catalogue so big the best options hide; sound on by default.

Balance

Personalised meditation

The closest to Tend’s promise in meditation: plans that adapt as you progress, with a very long free trial.

Price$11.99 / mo, $69.99 / yr, $399.99 lifetime; one-year free trial in promotions
Rating4.9 (95.4K App Store), 4.7 (35.8K Google Play)
Best forBeginners who want to be guided

Strengths

  • Personalises by meditation level, how often you sit and which skills you have mastered.
  • Audio adapts as you progress.
  • A small library that does not overwhelm beginners; praised for calm, simple guidance.
  • The long free trial lowers the barrier to start.

Weaknesses

  • About 60 meditations and 20 plans: small next to Headspace and Calm.
  • Billing complaints: charged after cancelling on the last trial day; “Free year is a lie”.
  • Unsubscribing is hard for some; streak tracking bugs.
  • Tailoring follows your skill, not your state today. Meditation only.
Tend takes: a small, curated library that adapts.
Tend avoids: any trial or billing flow that feels like a trap.

Insight Timer

Free library

The biggest free library in the category, a flexible timer, and a community of teachers.

PriceFree; MemberPlus $9.99 / mo, $59.99 / yr
Rating4.5 / 5 (Choosing Therapy, July 2026)
Scale170,000+ free meditations, plus yoga, mood tracking, workshops

Strengths

  • One of the largest free libraries anywhere; most people never need to pay.
  • A plain timer for people who already know what they want.
  • A reviewer praised its calm interface, free of manipulative streaks and gamification.
  • Mood tracking, mentoring and workshops beyond meditation.

Weaknesses

  • Content overload; the interface feels cluttered and discovery filters clunky.
  • Offline listening, the advanced player and most courses sit behind MemberPlus.
  • Uneven quality across tracks (reported in review summaries).
  • No guidance on what to do today; the choice is all yours.
Tend takes: no streaks, and a plain timer option.
Tend avoids: choice overload; we choose for you, and say why.

Othership

Breathwork · premium

Music-driven breathwork classes, sorted by the state you want to reach.

Price$17.99 / mo, $129.99 / yr; 7 and 14-day trials
Rating4.9 from about 2,100 U.S. App Store reviews
Library500+ classes; styles UP, DOWN, All Around, Body, Brain

Strengths

  • Sessions are named by the state they serve (up, down), not by technique, which matches how people think.
  • Music is matched to each session’s tone; T3 calls it a great introduction to breathwork.
  • Streaks, progress and a global community.
  • Very high rating from a sizeable base.

Weaknesses

  • Music can drown the vocal instructions (T3).
  • The most expensive app here; price is the recurring complaint.
  • Breath only: no stillness, herbs or reflection in the same flow.
Tend takes: name practices by the state they serve.
Tend avoids: sound that competes with guidance; ours is quiet ambient, off by choice.

Breathwrk

Breathwork · short sessions

More than 50 short breathing exercises for calm, sleep, energy and performance, many of them one or two minutes long.

PriceFree tier; Pro $9 / mo, $49 / yr, 7-day trial
Rating4.8 (16.6K App Store), 3.7 (692 Google Play)
FormatTimed inhales, holds and exhales with music, haptics and lessons

Strengths

  • Very short sessions fit into any day.
  • Adjustable voice, sounds and duration; progress tracking.
  • Haptics let you breathe with eyes closed; “Breathe With Friends”.
  • Users credit it with helping anxiety and relaxation.

Weaknesses

  • Limited free content and a mandatory sign-up before you can try anything.
  • Reports of hard cancellation and unexpected charges.
  • A redesign drew backlash: harder navigation, lost favourites, health-app sync issues, crashes (automated review summaries).
  • The 3.7 Google Play rating shows an uneven experience across platforms.
Tend takes: sessions measured in minutes, and a counted pattern on screen.
Tend avoids: sign-up before value; “Look around as Lena” works without an account.

Open

Breathwork, meditation, movement

A studio brand from Los Angeles with an app: breathwork, meditation and movement designed with experts in neurobiology.

PriceFree download; paid tiers not stated in the listings we found
Rating4.9 from 397 reviews in one store; small samples elsewhere
ChannelApp plus an in-person flagship studio

Strengths

  • Three practices in one product, close to Tend’s mix.
  • A credible, expert-led positioning.
  • An in-person studio gives the brand a physical home and community.

Weaknesses

  • Few independent reviews; hard to judge depth or pricing from outside.
  • The brand leans on the LA studio, which most users will never visit.
  • No herbal guidance and no visible day-by-day tailoring in its listing.
Tend takes: several practices in one ritual.
Tend avoids: expert claims without showing the reasoning in the product.

Stoic

Journal

A guided journal built around a morning to prepare and an evening to reflect, with mood tracking and habits.

PriceFree with in-app purchases; premium for AI and guided content
Rating4.8 from about 35K App Store reviews; Apple Editors’ Choice
PlatformsiPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch, Vision Pro

Strengths

  • A clear daily rhythm: prepare in the morning, reflect in the evening.
  • Prompts, mood tracking and habits in one place.
  • Strong ratings and Apple’s editorial backing.

Weaknesses

  • Apple only, with no web app.
  • Limited AI depth (a competitor’s comparison, so weigh it).
  • Reflection only; it does not lead into a practice.
Tend takes: morning and evening as two different moods of the same app.
Tend avoids: the journal as a separate chore; ours is one line at the end of the ritual.

HealisticMD

Herbal · AI companions

AI wellness companions for natural treatments, available around the clock, that track what seems to help you.

PriceFree with in-app purchases
PositioningA team of AI wellness companions
DisclaimerStates it does not provide medical advice

Strengths

  • Always-on conversation lowers the effort of asking.
  • Tracks over time what helps your body, a feedback loop most apps lack.
  • Free to start.

Weaknesses

  • The listing does not show how advice is sourced or checked against medicines.
  • A disclaimer stands in for safety design.
  • No breath, meditation or reflection around the remedy.
Tend takes: learn what helps over time.
Tend avoids: unsourced herbal advice; every herb shows its cautions, evidence and source.

Herbal Natural Care

Herbal · reference

An Ayurvedic herb database: medicinal herbs, what they are used for and how to prepare them.

PriceApp store listing
FormatSearchable reference
Best forLooking up a herb you already know

Strengths

  • Breadth of herbs with uses and preparation.
  • Useful for people who already know what they want.

Weaknesses

  • A reference, not a routine; it never tells you what to do today.
  • No personal safety checks against pregnancy, medicines or allergies.
Tend takes: preparation shown step by step.
Tend avoids: herbs without a person in mind.

Where the gap is

  • Nobody plans today from how you feel today. Headspace and Balance tailor to your history and skill; Othership sorts by the state you choose; none asks first and then decides.
  • Practices live in separate apps. Breath (Othership, Breathwrk), stillness (Headspace, Calm), reflection (Stoic) and herbs (HealisticMD) are each someone else’s product. Only Open combines practices, without herbs.
  • Herbal apps lack safety design. The two herbal apps either list herbs or talk about them; neither shows advice checked against medicines, pregnancy or allergies, with evidence and a source.
  • The category’s complaints are about trust, not features: billing traps (Balance, Breathwrk), sign-up walls (Breathwrk), clutter (Insight Timer), sound over guidance (Othership, Calm).
